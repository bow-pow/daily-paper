Superconductivity and non-Fermi liquid metals in a charge-1/3 anyon fluid

Zhengyan Darius Shi, T. Senthil
The Brief

This research explores a peculiar state of matter formed by doping a special type of insulator, revealing unexpected electronic behaviors.

The core idea involves how "anyons," exotic particles that aren't quite bosons or fermions, behave when added to a fractional Chern insulator. Instead of acting independently, these anyons interact and pair up in specific ways. One surprising outcome is a state called an "SC* state," where particles carrying twice the usual electron charge ($2e$) condense, coexisting with a fundamental type of quantum order called $\mathbb{Z}_2$ topological order. Another fascinating discovery is a "non-Fermi liquid" state at low doping, where electrons lose their familiar particle-like properties and behave in a more complex, fluid manner.

This work is interesting because it sheds light on exotic quantum phases of matter that could be realized in advanced materials like moiré superlattices, potentially leading to new technologies based on novel electronic properties.

Background

The study of exotic states of matter, particularly those exhibiting superconductivity and unusual electronic behavior, is a major frontier in physics. One area of intense interest is the "fractional quantum Hall effect" (FQHE), a phenomenon observed in 2D electron systems at very low temperatures and strong magnetic fields, where electrons behave as if they have fractional charges and exhibit peculiar "anyonic" statistics (meaning their quantum state changes in a non-trivial way when they are exchanged, unlike ordinary fermions or bosons). Recently, similar effects, known as "fractional Chern insulators" (FCIs), have been observed even without a strong external magnetic field, in materials like twisted transition metal dichalcogenides (e.g., MoTe$_2$) and moiré graphene. When these insulating states are "doped" with additional electrons, they can form a fluid of these fractionally charged anyons. The central question this research addresses is: what kind of fascinating new electronic states can emerge from this fluid of mobile, fractionally charged anyons?

What they did

This paper delves into the theoretical possibilities arising from doping a specific type of FCI, the $\nu=2/3$ Jain state, with charge-1/3 anyons. The researchers employ a powerful theoretical framework called the "composite fermion" (CF) description. In this picture, the fundamental charged particles (electrons) are imagined to bind to magnetic flux lines, forming composite particles that behave like simpler particles in an effective magnetic field. When the $\nu=2/3$ FCI is doped, the charge-1/3 anyons are described as further fractionalizing into three "flavors" of secondary composite fermions (sCFs). These sCFs interact via emergent gauge fields. The core of the work involves analyzing the instabilities of this sCF fluid. Specifically, the paper investigates what happens when these sCFs try to "pair up" (a mechanism that leads to superconductivity) and also explores a scenario where the interactions between the sCFs possess an approximate SU(3) symmetry. To do this, they use sophisticated theoretical tools, including effective field theories, Chern-Simons theory, and renormalization group analysis, to describe the complex interactions and potential ground states.

What they found

The research uncovers several distinct and exotic electronic phases. Firstly, they identify a "flavor-symmetric paired state" which, depending on the strength of the pairing, leads to two types of "SC*" states. These are not just superconductors but exotic states where superconductivity coexists with a residual topological order (a form of order that doesn't break symmetries but has inherent quantum properties). In the weak pairing regime, this leads to an SC* state with a chiral central charge $c_- = 7/2$, and in the strong pairing regime, it results in an SC* state with $c_- = 2$. The paper also shows how these states are related to other theoretical descriptions in the field. Crucially, they propose that as the system transitions from the insulating FCI to these superconducting states, the localized anyons transform into "Bogoliubov quasiparticles" (a type of particle in superconductors) rather than vortices. Secondly, at very low doping, where an approximate SU(3) symmetry is present, the system is predicted to realize a "non-Fermi liquid" state called a $\mathbb{Z}_3$ Orthogonal Metal. This state is characterized by three pockets of charge-1/3 fermions, but unlike conventional metals, it lacks sharp electron quasiparticles, a hallmark of non-Fermi liquid behavior.

Why it matters

This work provides a theoretical roadmap for understanding the complex and potentially groundbreaking electronic states that can emerge from doped fractional Chern insulators, materials that are now being experimentally realized. The identification of SC* states, where superconductivity coexists with topological order, offers new avenues for exploring topological quantum computing. The prediction of a $\mathbb{Z}_3$ Orthogonal Metal, a simple yet exotic non-Fermi liquid, is particularly exciting as it presents a concrete target for experimental observation in systems like twisted MoTe$_2$. The paper also clarifies how different theoretical approaches fit into a unified framework, offering a more coherent picture of this rich field. The findings have direct implications for interpreting current experiments and guiding future research in the search for novel quantum phases of matter.
