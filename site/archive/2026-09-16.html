Differential Addition on Twisted Edwards Curves

Seyed Gholamhossein Hosseini, Reza Rezaeian Farashahi
The Brief

This research introduces faster ways to perform a specific type of calculation on a type of mathematical curve called a twisted Edwards curve.

The core idea is to develop new formulas for "differential addition," which means adding two points on the curve when you already know their difference. This is crucial for speeding up a process called "scalar multiplication," a fundamental operation in cryptography. The new formulas achieve this efficiency by reducing the number of expensive arithmetic operations (multiplications, squarings, and multiplications by a constant) needed.

This work is interesting because these curves and operations are the backbone of many modern encryption systems, so improving their speed can lead to more efficient and secure digital communication and transactions.

Background

In modern cryptography, systems like those used for secure online transactions or digital signatures rely on mathematical problems that are very hard to solve. One such problem involves "elliptic curves," which are special types of curves with interesting mathematical properties. A crucial operation in elliptic curve cryptography is "scalar multiplication," which is like repeatedly adding a point on the curve to itself many times. This operation is fundamental to how these cryptographic systems work, but it can be computationally expensive. Researchers are constantly looking for ways to speed up these calculations without compromising security. A particular concern is "side-channel attacks," where attackers try to glean secret information by observing how long a computation takes or how much power it consumes. Certain algorithms, like the "Montgomery ladder," are designed to resist these attacks by performing a fixed sequence of operations, regardless of the secret information. This requires efficient ways to perform "point addition" (adding two different points) and "point doubling" (adding a point to itself) in a specific way that works well with the Montgomery ladder.

What they did

This paper focuses on a specific type of elliptic curve called "twisted Edwards curves." These curves are popular in cryptography because they have a very efficient way of adding points. The core of this work is to develop new formulas for "differential addition" and "doubling" on these curves. Differential addition is a special kind of point addition where you know the *difference* between two points, not just the points themselves. This is exactly what's needed for the Montgomery ladder. The researchers derived these formulas by using a specific mathematical representation of points on the curve called a "w-coordinate." They explored several different "w-coordinate" functions, each leading to different sets of formulas. The goal was to find formulas that are as fast as possible, measured by the number of basic arithmetic operations (multiplication, squaring, and multiplication by a constant) needed. They also considered different ways of representing points (affine vs. projective coordinates) and how the "completeness" of the formulas (whether they work for all possible inputs) affects their efficiency.

What they found

The paper presents several new sets of formulas for differential addition and doubling on twisted Edwards curves. The key results are the reported costs of these operations. For instance, they provide formulas with a cost of 5M+4S+1D when the "difference point" is in affine form, where M is a field multiplication, S is a field squaring, and D is a multiplication by a constant. They also offer faster formulas for specific subfamilies of twisted Edwards curves, achieving costs like 3M+7S+1D and even 3M+6S+3D in certain cases. Notably, they highlight that some of these formulas are "complete," meaning they work for all valid inputs, which is important for cryptographic security. They also compare their findings to existing methods for other types of elliptic curves, showing improvements in efficiency for certain scenarios.

Why it matters

Faster and more efficient cryptographic operations mean that secure communication and digital signatures can be performed more quickly, which is crucial for resource-constrained devices like smart cards or in high-throughput applications. The development of "differential addition" formulas specifically tailored for twisted Edwards curves and the Montgomery ladder algorithm is important because it directly addresses the need for secure and efficient scalar multiplication in cryptography. By providing optimized formulas, this research contributes to making elliptic curve cryptography more practical and performant, especially in contexts where speed and resistance to side-channel attacks are paramount. The specific cost reductions, like moving from 5M+4S+1D to 3M+6S+3D for certain cases, represent significant practical gains in computational efficiency.
